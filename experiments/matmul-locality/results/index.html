The experiment

01 · Straightforward loops

For each output Cᵢⱼ, reduce AᵢₚBₚⱼ in ascending p. A is traversed along rows while B is read down columns.

02 · Reorder the loops

Hold Aᵢₚ and stream across row p of B and row i of C. Output writes increase, but both inner-loop arrays are contiguous.

03 · Block the working set

Visit 32 × 32 submatrices, retaining the same reduction order. The hypothesis is better reuse at larger sizes; extra loops and short spans may lose at smaller sizes.

04 · Vectorize adjacent columns

Explicit NEON computes two output columns together. Scalar auto-vectorization and fused multiply-add are disabled in all handwritten kernels to isolate this step.

Accelerate, when included, is a vendor reference—not another handwritten step. Its reduction order, internal allocations and thread behavior are outside this harness’s control. One thread is requested. No claim is made about its instruction set.

Correctness and allocation boundaries

Small and awkward rectangular shapes are compared with an independent exact rational oracle, including empty dimensions, odd SIMD tails, tile boundaries, cancellation and mixed scales. Rounding is checked against a bound proportional to Σ|aᵢₚbₚⱼ|.

Timing inputs are bounded integers divided by 16. Every output entry is checked against an exact integer reference before and after each batch. A three-entry checksum is consumed during every timed call. The same input buffers are reused, so this is a warm-cache study, not streaming throughput.

What the measurements do—and don’t—establish

A fixed tile size is deliberately retained even when it loses. This study does not establish a universal best kernel, does not compare programming languages, and does not change library defaults. Nominal GFLOP/s uses 2MKN operations and includes output initialization and harness overhead.
