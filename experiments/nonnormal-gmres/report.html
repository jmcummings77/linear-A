Nonnormal GMRES and graph controls

Eigenvalues alone do not predict restarted GMRES convergence. These effective GMRES operators all have eigenvalues 1 and 1, yet a one-step restart can make slow progress or stagnate. Keeping two Krylov directions solves every captured case in at most two steps.

The effective matrix is B = [[1, 2s], [0, 1]]. Increasing s changes its departure from normality while its spectrum stays fixed. The one-step restart discards the Krylov space at each step; the two-step method retains the degree-two polynomial that annihilates B.

Each case is repeated with A = B diag(1,8) and right Jacobi preconditioning. This recovers the same effective operator B, and the paired residual histories agree within their rounding allowance. This constructed equivalence does not claim that Jacobi improves a general nonsymmetric system.

Why the numerical range adds information

The complex numerical range W(B) is the disk centered at 1 with radius |s|. For p(z) = 1 − z, the maximum |p(z)| on that disk is |s|, while the operator norm is exactly 2|s|. The existing SVD checks this norm; the general eigensolver checks the repeated eigenvalue.

‖I − B‖₂ = 2|s|   and   (I − B)² = 0

This family attains the scalar Crouzeix constant 2 whenever s ≠ 0. It illustrates the scalar specialization of the cited complete inequality. It does not test the general theorem or implement a new GMRES algorithm.

For step j within a restart cycle, the comparison envelope is ‖r_cycle_start‖₂ min(1, 2|s|ʲ). It uses a candidate polynomial and the constant polynomial's no-progress bound. It is deliberately loose at j = 2, where the actual operator polynomial vanishes. When |s| ≥ 1, the disk contains zero and this envelope gives no guaranteed progress.

The envelope is an exact-arithmetic comparison with a scale-dependent floating-point allowance. It is neither a certified error bound nor the stopping rule. The solver continues to decide convergence from the true residual of the original system.

A checkable two-step example

At s = 1/4 with no preconditioner, both restart lengths first reach x₁ = (0, 4/5), leaving r₁ = (−2/5, 1/5). Restarting after that step gives x₂ = (−32/65, 68/65) and r₂ = (−2/65, −3/65). Keeping the Krylov space instead reaches the exact solution (−1/2, 1) in the second step.

The first two restarted projections are checked with exact rational line minimizations: α = (rᵀBr)/‖Br‖₂², x_next = x + αr. This oracle does not reuse the solver's Arnoldi or Givens implementation.

Graph controls with known spectra

The companion experiment uses the Petersen graph, the triangular prism, and larger classical prisms. Their known adjacency spectra provide independently checkable sparse inputs. With adjacency H and degree 3, A = 3.25I − H is a shifted graph Laplacian. Its energy is ¼ Σxᵢ² + Σ_edges(xᵢ − xⱼ)², which is strictly positive for every nonzero vector.

The scalable prism is C_m × K₂, with 2m vertices and adjacency eigenvalues 2 cos(2πk/m) ± 1. Its nonconstant gap closes as m grows. The fixed shift keeps the full-space condition number bounded: for even m it is 25, including the eigenvalue 1/4 of the constant mode. A closing unshifted graph gap must not be presented as an unbounded condition number for this shifted system.

Jacobi is scalar scaling here because every diagonal entry is 3.25. It cannot improve the exact-arithmetic condition number; floating-point stopping steps can still differ. The right-hand side is assembled from edge differences of a deterministic nonconstant dyadic solution, independently of CSR multiplication.

The Petersen graph and triangular prism are nonbipartite Ramanujan examples. Arbitrarily large prisms are not a Ramanujan family. These classical controls do not implement the cited paper's new graph construction, and they are outside the library's structured-grid multigrid contract.

Method and reproducibility

GMRES starts at zero with b = (0,1), relative tolerance @@RTOL@@, zero absolute tolerance, and an iteration limit of @@LIMIT@@. All 20 trajectories retain actual stop reasons, restart boundaries, iterates, true residuals, and projected residual estimates. The two scaled variants use the same effective Krylov operator.

Every true residual is recomputed from the explicit two-entry expression for b − Ax. The absolute comparison allowance is 256 ε (1 + |x₀| + |2s D x₁| + |D x₁|), where D is 1 or 8. This allowance is scoped to these bounded examples. Late stagnation decisions and last-bit residuals can vary with Python and platform arithmetic.
