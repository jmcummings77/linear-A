What creates fill—and what gets checked?
At pivot k, connect every pair of remaining neighbors of k. New edges become entries of the lower factor even if a numerical cancellation later gives zero. Analysis uses the union of the stored pattern and its transpose; numerical factorization requires exact symmetry and a positive finite pivot at every step. A missing diagonal is analyzed structurally and rejected numerically when its pivot is nonpositive.
The numerical phase uses sparse row intersections to compute L[i,j] = (A[i,j] − Σ L[i,k]L[j,k]) / L[j,j], then the square root of the remaining diagonal. Forward and backward substitution reuse L for new right-hand sides. This page independently checks ‖A − LLᵀ‖F / ‖A‖F and ‖b − Ax‖₂ / ‖b‖₂, after restoring original coordinates.
RCM reduces bandwidth heuristically; it need not minimize fill or runtime. These are educational scalar implementations without supernodes, diagonal shifts or pivoting. See Cornell’s sparse factorization notes for symbolic elimination and fill.
