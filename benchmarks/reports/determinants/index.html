What these numbers mean
A small residual says the computed solution almost satisfies these equations. It does not say the solution is insensitive to the inputs. Backward error is ‖b − Ax‖∞ / (‖A‖∞ ‖x‖∞ + ‖b‖∞), with a zero denominator reported as zero. Reciprocal condition is 1 / (‖A‖∞ ‖A⁻¹‖∞): a value near zero indicates sensitivity. It is computed through solves, costs O(n³), and is not a certified bound. The solution-change figure compares two computed answers; it is not forward error against an exact answer. SVD uses the reciprocal 2-norm condition σmin / σmax instead, with zero for a zero spectrum. Its rank counts σ / σmax strictly greater than the chosen cutoff; the retained condition uses only those values. Rank zero returns the zero solution. Truncation solves a nearby lower-rank problem, so its residual against the original equations can increase. Ridge uses σ / (σ² + λ) in every positive singular direction, without a hard cutoff when λ > 0. Its displayed rank and condition describe A, not the regularized problem. Zero λ in the library uses the default minimum-norm cutoff; this slider covers positive λ. Backward error still measures the original equations, not optimality of the penalized objective. These are different norm conventions and should not be compared as identical estimates. QR rejects numerical rank deficiency; Cholesky requires exact symmetry and positive computed pivots. Diagnostics also have floating-point rounding error. These live values are separate from the saved performance measurements above.

Eigenvalues & eigenvectors of M
        

        
M v = λ v. Real eigenvectors keep their line under M; negative λ reverses direction and zero λ collapses it. A complex conjugate pair spans a real invariant plane, drawn as an ellipse using the real and imaginary parts of its vector. These describe the fixed input M during animation. General eigenvectors need not be orthogonal or independent; defective matrices may repeat a direction.
