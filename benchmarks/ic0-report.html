Why can IC(0) fail on a positive-definite matrix?
The breakdown example is A = 1.5 I + B, where B² = 2 I. Its eigenvalues are 1.5 ± √2, both positive. In natural order, the incomplete LDLᵀ pivots are 3/2, 5/6, 3/10 and −5/2. Dropping fill changes the factorization enough to lose a positive pivot. Full Cholesky succeeds. No diagonal shift is applied silently.
CG assumes A is symmetric positive definite. An IC(0) factor with positive pivots defines an SPD preconditioner, but does not certify that A itself is SPD. Every displayed converged solution and every captured residual is checked independently in original coordinates. Orderings can change both the approximation and iteration count.
Live setup and solve times are single observations, with final cleanup and independent checks excluded. Ordering and coordinate preparation are reported separately. Zero duration means below browser clock resolution, not a failure.
